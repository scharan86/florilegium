# Electronic Business

- Umbrella term that encompasses all business activities or processes conducted using the internet.
- internal operations, supply chain management, R&D, manufacturing, logistics, customer service,
etc.
- E-commerce is the transactional or commercial component of e-business.

# Electronic Commerce

- Commercial activities (buy/sell) over the internet.
- can be for goods or services.
- No single universally agreed definition. 
- Some countries extend it very broadly and some narrow and some medium. 
- Intersection point: "Buying and selling over the internet."

>[!note] UK gov's definition of e-commerce is broad:
> It includes information exchanged at any stage of the supply chain, activities within an
> organization, between businesses, and pre and post-sales activities. 

## Components 

1. **Buy-side** e-com: Transactions involving procurement of resources by an **org** from its suppliers.
2. **Sell-side** e-com: Transactions involving the sale of products to customers.

- When Amazon buys from a supplier, that's Amazon's buy-side and the supplier's sell-side.
- It's the same transaction viewed from two different perspectives.
- Every e-com transaction has both sides simultaneously. They form a **complete commercial cycle**
together. 


>[!note] Intranet and Extranet
> Intranet = private network operated and maintained by an org that's restricted to its employees.
> Extranet = A private network that's extended beyond a company for **selective and controlled
> access** to trusted outsiders like customers, suppliers, distributors, etc.
> Extranet example: a company giving its suppliers login access to view their inventory. 

## Types of Sell-side e-commerce (online presence)
Four main types: 
1. **Transactional** e-commerce web-sites
2. Service oriented **relationship-building** websites
3. **Brand-building** web-sites
4. **Portal, Publisher, Media** web-sites.

**Reasoning**: Not every product is suitable for sale online.
