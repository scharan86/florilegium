>[!Definition]
> Data that has all the properties of [[Ordinal Data|ordinal data]] and the distance between values are **meaningful and equal**. 
- It doesn't have a true zero, zero is arbitrary but it doesn't mean absence.
- It's a type of [[Continuous Variable|continuous variables]] (quantitative).

>[!example] Temperature in Centigrade or Fareheit
>- The difference between 20°C and 30°C is the same as between 30°C and 40°C — intervals are equal - But 0°C does not mean "no heat" — it's just the freezing point of water
> - Therefore, 40°C is _not_ twice as hot as 20°C — this ratio is meaningless
> - (In Kelvin, zero truly means no thermal energy — making it a ratio scale)
>   
> A zero IQ score doesn't mean zero intelligence. 
> Year 0 is not "no time". (not true zero)

## Properties
1. Equal intervals throughout the scale.
2. Zero is defined point on the scale, not true absence.
3. Ratios are meaningless  due to the arbitrary zero.
