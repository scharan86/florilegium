## Incompleteness ([[Underspecification]])
 Natural language only carries partial information about what they describe. Listeners must fill-in the gaps using context, world knowledge and [[inference]].
## Context Dependent ([[Deixis]] & [[Pragmatics]])
 Expressions aren't **static**. Their meaning changes based on physical settings, context, speaker intent and shared background knowledge.
## Open-Endedness ([[Productivity]])
 Natural languages aren't closed systems. New words, phrases, idioms, and meanings emerge continuously.
## Paraphrase and Synonymy ([[Redundancy]])
The same thing can be expressed in multiple syntactic and lexical ways.
## Structural Ambiguity
A single sentence can have multiple valid parse trees.
e.g. "I saw the man with the telescope"
## Lexical Ambiguity ([[Polysemy]] & [[Homonymy]])
A single word can have multiple distinct senses.
## [[Anaphora and Coreference]] 
Pronouns and referring expressions point back to entities mentioned earlier.
## Non-Literal Language 
Metaphor, irony, sarcasm and idioms.
e.g. "This project is a minefield"
## [[Presupposition]]
Sentences carry background assumptions that are taken for granted rather than explicitly stated.
e.g. "Have you stopped cheating?" (relationship, diet, academics, etc)

These 9 features collectively define why NLP is hard...
