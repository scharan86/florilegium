In linguistics, morphology is the study of **internal construction** (structure) of words.

>[!example]
>In English,
>The world "replacement" can be decomposed into "re", "place", and "ment".
## Core Idea
Words aren't atomic, they can often be decomposed into smaller meaningful units (*morphemes*). 

> A *morpheme* is the smallest unit of meaning in a language.

## Types of Morphemes
1. **Stems**:  part with the core lexical meaning. E.g. "place" in "replacement".
2. **Prefix**:  Part that is added before the stem. E.g. "re" in "replacement".
3. **Suffix**: Added after the stem. E.g. "ment" in "replacement".

>[!question] What does it do computationally?
>1. Produces [[Lemmas|lemmas]] 
>2. Identifies [[POS]] through suffixes
>3. Handles unknown words by decomposing them
>4. Reduces vocabulary size by collapsing inflected variants.
