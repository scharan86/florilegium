## Notes

|Approach|Goal|
|---|---|
|Cognitive modeling|Think like a human — mirror actual human thought processes|
|Laws of thought|Think rationally — reason correctly using logic and formal rules|
|Turing test approach|Act like a human — produce behavior indistinguishable from a human|
|Rational agent approach|Act rationally — take actions that maximize the expected outcome|

Cognitive modeling specifically requires grounding AI in psychological and neuroscientific evidence of how humans actually think — not just producing the right output, but producing it the same _way_ a human would. GPS (General Problem Solver) by Newell and Simon is the classic example, built explicitly to mirror human problem-solving steps.

The definition you stated earlier falls more under the **rational agent** or **Turing test** camp — it cares about what the system _does_, not whether the internal process resembles human cognition.
