# R&D vs Innovation

| Aspect            | R&D                                            | Innovation                                   |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**    | Systematic investigation to gain new knowledge | Applying new ideas to create value           |
| **Focus**         | Discovery and experimentation                  | Implementation and commercialization         |
| **Goal**          | Generate knowledge or technology               | Solve problems or capture market opportunity |
| **Output**        | Patents, papers, prototypes                    | Products, services, processes                |
| **Risk**          | High — outcome is uncertain                    | Moderate — builds on known concepts          |
| **Time horizon**  | Long-term                                      | Short to medium-term                         |
| **Nature**        | Exploratory                                    | Purposeful and applied                       |
| **Who drives it** | Scientists, researchers                        | Entrepreneurs, product teams                 |
| **Relation**      | Often precedes innovation                      | May or may not require R&D                   |

In short: R&D creates new knowledge; innovation turns knowledge (new or existing) into value.
- **Research** — Systematic inquiry to generate new knowledge or understanding.
- **Development** — Applying research findings to build tangible products, systems, or processes.
- **Innovation** — Turning developed ideas or existing knowledge into real-world value, whether commercial or societal.
So the natural flow is: **Research → Development → Innovation**, though in practice it's rarely that linear.

>[!note] Research & Research Problem
>Research is a formal and systematic process of generating, refining, or evaluating knowledge through rigorous methodology, aimed at answering specific questions or solving problems. Its scope may be limited to a single domain or extend across multiple disciplines in an interdisciplinary manner.
>
A research problem is the identification and articulation of an unresolved question, gap, or inconsistency in knowledge within a specific domain, which can be addressed through systematic inquiry and whose resolution holds academic or practical significance.
